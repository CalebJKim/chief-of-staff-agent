# Northstar Customer Recovery

Northstar's deployment needs a clearer transition from implementation to ongoing support. The teams do not yet have a shared checklist bringing together configuration details, known limitations, and the route for customer questions.

Without that shared record, a question can move between teams even when each team believes it has completed its part. The goal is an understandable support route and a handoff the customer can accept.

## Proposed recovery sequence
1. Assemble the deployment profile: intended workflow, configuration, dependencies, limitations, and customer-facing contacts.
2. Separate unfinished implementation questions from incidents and routine service requests.
3. Review responsibilities with implementation and support. Keep disputed responsibilities visible.
4. Walk through an access problem and an unavailable-document problem to see whether the routing works.
5. Ask the customer to confirm the support path and remaining limitations before closing the handoff.

These are pending steps, not completed recovery results.

## What a useful update should say
Explain the customer impact, what changed, what the customer can safely do now, what remains uncertain, and what triggers the next update. An internal activity list does not show that the customer experience has improved.

If a workaround adds work for the customer, explain its limits and who supports it. Keep immediate containment, investigation, permanent correction, and customer confirmation distinct.

## Conditions for closure
The customer should know where to ask for help. Support should have enough context to route the case. Implementation should have a defined route for questions that need design knowledge. There must also be a way to return an incomplete handoff for correction.

Use [[enterprise-support-model]] for the checklist and [[openshell]] for relevant technical dependencies. Confirm the owner and next decision date through [[weekly-leadership-staff]], then record accepted commitments in [[decision-log]]. Longer-term improvements should not displace the immediate recovery work.
