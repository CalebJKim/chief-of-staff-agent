# Workforce Plan

This plan asks which skills and responsibilities are needed to deliver the operating plan, and how to cover the gaps. More headcount is one possible answer, not the starting assumption.

## Capabilities being examined
Enterprise onboarding needs people who can turn a customer's intended workflow into a clear deployment scope, acceptance checks, and support handoff.

Partner enablement needs people who can maintain integration examples, explain prerequisites, and coordinate technical questions across organizations.

These needs do not automatically mean two new jobs. The same senior reviewers may also be needed for documentation and developer education in [[q4-operating-plan]].

## Compare coverage options
| Response | When worth considering | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Develop an existing employee | Capability can grow within the available horizon | Requires protected coaching and practice |
| Reassign or share expertise | Demand is concentrated or temporary | Moves capacity away from other work |
| Simplify or defer scope | The outcome can be achieved in stages | Changes the timing or breadth of benefits |
| Recruit | A durable capability gap remains | Search and onboarding consume time |

## What to bring to the review
Describe the work, current coverage, dependency on individual specialists, and the consequence of leaving a gap. Include the time needed to recruit, train, and review work.

A possible development assignment is coordinating a deployment acceptance packet or a partner example through review. [[executive-recruiting]] holds proposed role and interview questions; [[leadership-succession]] covers continuity and development.

Keep private individual assessments separate from the wider workforce view. No candidate, readiness judgment, or hiring approval is recorded here. Confirm the next decision through [[weekly-leadership-staff]] and keep accepted commitments in [[decision-log]].
