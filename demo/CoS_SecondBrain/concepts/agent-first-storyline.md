# Agent-First Storyline

The proposed keynote story presents RTX Spark through useful work a person can do, rather than starting with hardware specifications or a list of AI terms. The audience should understand the problem before hearing how the technology helps.

## A simple way to tell the story
1. A person receives a request or needs to finish a task.
2. The proposed workflow gathers relevant information.
3. It prepares a result the person can inspect, such as a draft or suggested change.
4. The person makes the decision that still needs their judgment.

This is a structure for explaining a candidate workflow, not a promise that every product supports every step.

## What supports the story
The [[hermes-partner-program|partner program]] can explain how different parts of a workflow connect. [[openshell|OpenShell]] can explain execution controls. The recorded [[inference-performance-claims|product evidence]] can support specific statements within its approved scope.

For each example, answer: What did the person ask? What information was used? What appeared on screen? What remained for the person to review? Those answers are more useful than terms such as orchestration without an explanation.

## What still needs approval
The proposed story and [[ifa-demo-slate|demo list]] go to the [[rtx-spark-executive-review|executive review]]. A clear narrative does not mean the selection has been approved.

If a live example is not appropriate, an annotated walkthrough can explain the idea. Label it as a walkthrough during the presentation. It is not evidence of live execution, unrestricted autonomy, or new product performance.
