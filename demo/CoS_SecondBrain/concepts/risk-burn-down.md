# Risk Burn-Down

Risk burn-down tracks whether a program's risks are becoming less serious. It ranks them by consequence, how soon they could matter, and confidence in the proposed response.

## Measuring progress
Finishing a task does not necessarily reduce the risk. A contingency plan may need resources that are still unavailable, and a completed test may cover only one type of failure.

Explain what changed in the likelihood, consequence, or ability to recover. Identify the owner and the next decision needed.

## Reviewing what remains
Ask what would make the risk urgent, what evidence supports the response, and what fallback is available. Monitoring a risk is different from an authorized decision to accept it.

If a tracking item is closed, retain the reason and conditions behind that acceptance. Use this approach in [[operational-resilience|operational resilience]] and [[board-readiness-program|the board readiness program]], checking current evidence before changing a risk assessment.
