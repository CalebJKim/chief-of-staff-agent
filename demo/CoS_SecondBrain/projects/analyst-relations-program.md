# Analyst Relations Program

The program prepares discussions with analysts about strategy, differentiation, and roadmap direction. Use the briefing to learn how the audience understands the work and what evidence they still need.

## Briefing under discussion
Start with the problem of moving from an isolated answer to work a person can inspect and control. Explain the proposed user journey, what can be delegated, and what still requires a person's decision.

Prepare a workflow diagram, short terminology guide, reviewed narrative, and list of questions. Keep externally shareable facts separate from roadmap topics that need disclosure review.

## Questions worth asking
- What category would you put this workflow in, and what would you compare it with?
- What would distinguish an appealing demo from something ready for wider use?
- Where are the adoption obstacles: integration, governance, support, or user understanding?
- Which parts of the explanation depend on unfamiliar internal terms?

Different analysts may need different supporting detail. Leave room for disagreement instead of treating interest as endorsement.

## After the discussion
Separate clarification requests, challenges to the approach, and factual claims needing research. Record unanswered questions and who can investigate them.

Coordinate with [[communications-readiness]], [[competitive-response-deepseek]], and [[global-analyst-council]]. Confirm the owner and next milestone through [[weekly-leadership-staff]] and record commitments in [[decision-log]]. No briefing outcome, published opinion, product benchmark, or analyst endorsement is assumed in this plan.
