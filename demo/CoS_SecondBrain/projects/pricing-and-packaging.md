# Pricing and Packaging

This project decides what customers would buy together, how they would pay, and who would support the offering. A simple price can still hide a complicated customer experience if the included services and limits are unclear.

## Compare the whole offer
For each option, describe the unit of purchase, included capability, support, upgrade path, and partner responsibilities. Explain what is needed to begin and what makes cost change later.

A lower-entry package may help customers try the offering but create a difficult upgrade if essential operating features are missing. A broad bundle can simplify buying while charging some customers for things they do not need. These are design scenarios, not proposed prices.

## Evidence for a decision
Bring customer-segment needs, alternatives, delivery costs, and assumptions about partner economics. Distinguish research into what someone might pay from a commercial commitment.

Consider how discounts, exceptions, and renewals would work. A package should not depend on unpriced support effort or an unclear obligation passed to another team.
